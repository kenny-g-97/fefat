# ADR-0024: The store's records at another offset, id, or trade date refuse a file

Decision log: [D-108](../masterplan/DECISIONS.md), 2026-11-19

## Context

An import merges a file into the partitions it overlaps (ADR-0021): a record at a place the store lacks is added, one the store holds there with the same values is already held, and an instant the two dispute refuses the file unless the import is told that the file's records stand. Three ways a wrong file could still enter the store were left open by that rule (D-99, D-100, D-103):

- **A file read under the wrong time zone or bar stamping.** Every import's calendar check refuses records that run whole or half hours, or a bar, late or early against their sessions (step 1.10, ADR-0022), but it cannot test a file of regular hours only, a thin one, or one of a calendar without breaks, whose records moved by an hour still fit their sessions. Such a file's bars, and its prints stamped to the second, land on the store's instants and read as differing, which `--differing take-file` takes, or on instants the store lacks, which the merge adds by default.
- **A contract's new partition under another `instrument_id`.** The merge refuses a file that gives a stored partition another id, but a contract's new month is compared with nothing, so a file that names its instruments otherwise than the files before it, by its own id column where they had none, writes it, and the contract loads as two series: the session grid is built by `instrument_id` (`fefat.market.bars`, `signal_grid`).
- **A calendar change between two imports.** A record keeps the trade date its import's session table gave it, and the merge compares records within one partition, so after a correction of a holiday, a former close, or a saved instrument's hours, a file's record can be dated otherwise than the store dates the same instant: in another partition, stored a second time, or in the same partition under another trade date, where taking the file splits one session over two trade dates.

A merge cannot be taken back, while since step 5.18 a provider's data of a root can be deleted whole and its files imported again (ADR-0023). Measured read-only on the builder's store at the plan, 25 million records of six 1-minute datasets and IBKR's prints: no contract holds two ids, no two neighbouring partitions of a contract lie out of order in time, and no record is dated otherwise than its calendar dates it now. Months of it moved an hour late or a bar early match the stored bars at that offset in every record the store holds a bar beside, while 988 correct files built from about 250 of its months, a month carried on, a missing session filled, every second or third bar filled, match at most 36 of 1,015 records (3.5 %) at any of 314 offsets; a session of a million MNQ prints moved by one or six hours matches 2,000 of a sample of 2,000 by place.

## Decision

Three refusals that no option lifts, in the check pass of both importers and of their previews, before anything is written, each naming its finding in counts, dates, instants, or ids, never a price (D-21), and both ways out: the instrument's page in the app or `fefat data delete <provider> <root>`, then importing the files again.

- **Records that are the stored ones at another offset.** A file's records the store does not hold, those it would add as well as those that differ, are compared with the stored records of their partition and of their contract's stored partitions beside it, since a day session of prints read nine hours late is dated to the next trade date while its originals lie under the one before. At each offset a misread file shows, every half hour up to 26 hours late or early, the most two time zones lie apart, and for bars shorter than a day one bar either way, alone or on top of those half hours, a record matches where the store holds, at its instant less the offset and its place in that instant, a record with its instrument id, prices, and volume, or for a print its instrument id, price, size, and side: the columns every import fills. A sample of at most 2,000 records a partition, every k-th, is compared. A dataset is refused where at one offset at least 10 records match and they are at least half of those beside which the store holds a record there. Daily bars, dated by the date they name, and records of other schemas are not compared. The rule speaks before the refusals whose advice would lead away from it: a back-adjusted series that shares no bar with the store, and prints without a sequence that dispute their file's first second.
- **A contract's new partition under another id.** A partition the store does not hold carries only the ids of its contract's stored partitions beside it, the nearest below and above by key, and of the file's earlier partitions of that contract; where it has neither, nothing is compared.
- **A record the store holds under another trade date.** In a partition of bars, each of the file's records carries a trade date no earlier than the stored record's at or before its instant and no later than the stored record's at or after it, and a daily bar's trade date stands at one open. Across partitions, a partition's records lie after the last instant of its contract's stored partition below and before the first instant of the one above, since under one calendar trade dates never decrease along the clock.
- **The check after a calendar change.** `fefat data verify-trade-dates [<root>]` lists per dataset, contract, and stored trade date the records whose instant lies outside that date's session and the break after it, and the daily bars that do not open at their session's open, with the trade date the calendar gives them now, and exits with 1 when it lists any. Such records are dated again by deleting the provider's data of the root and importing its files again; nothing rewrites stored files.

## Consequences

- No stored record moves and nothing that reads the store changes: the rules refuse files, and the check reads.
- An import reads, besides the partitions its file overlaps, its contract's stored partitions beside each of them, once a pass, for their ids and span, and where it adds or disputes records near them, the instants a sample asks of them. A preview of the builder's MNQ June 2024 export read again took 1.5 s before the rules and after them, every record already held, and the offset rule takes 0.03 to 0.07 s on a dense month whose every bar differs. The check reads the builder's 25 million records in under 3 s.
- A file whose records the store holds at an offset cannot come in beside them under any option. Where the stored data is the misread side, it is deleted and imported again, from the trader's own files, since a delete also removes FEFAT's copies of them under `raw`.
- A file that repairs a stretch the store holds displaced is refused like a misread one, since its records equal the stored ones an hour away; the builder's NinjaTrader history holds such an hour on 2025-11-02 (`BACKLOG.md`). The repair is a delete, then the repaired export imported first and the others after it, the old export of that contract then refused, as it should be.
- A misread file with fewer than 10 matching records still merges, as the three hourly bars an update shares with the store would: a lower floor would refuse thin correct files, in which one or two records the store holds beside them match by chance. So does one whose records all lie beyond its contract's stored partitions beside them, or at an offset of a quarter hour.
- A contract the store holds under one id in its trades and another in its bars still loads as two series: the rule compares one dataset (`BACKLOG.md`). A listing ten years ahead under an expired contract's symbol, which the DBN importer names a decade early (`fefat.market.contracts.expiry_month`), now refuses its file.
- After a calendar change, a file beside a stretch the store dates otherwise, in order and sharing no instant with it, is taken, and the session lies under two trade dates until the check's list is acted on.
- The code is `fefat.market.importers.stored_offset` and `stored_neighbours`, `partition_merge`, `partition_buffers`, `file_import`, `fefat.market.trade_date_check`, and `fefat data verify-trade-dates`.

## How we would know it is wrong

If a vendor's correction, a longer export, or a file that fills missing sessions were refused as the stored records at an offset; if a file read hours or a bar off, beside more than a handful of stored records, merged; if a contract loaded as two series after an import, or a session lay under two trade dates without the check listing it; or if calendar changes proved common enough that deleting and importing again was too heavy.

## Status

Accepted; recorded at the plan of step 5.16. It amends [ADR-0021](0021-imports-merge-into-the-partitions-they-overlap.md): records that are the stored ones at another offset refuse a file whatever the import is told, as do a partition new to the store under another id than its contract's and a record the store holds under another trade date.
