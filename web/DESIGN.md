# FEAT design system

The one design system of the web app (D-28). It applies the Contemporary skill (`.claude/skills/contemporary-typeui/`), the reference the design brief follows exactly (D-88), to FEAT, and records each place where FEAT departs from the skill, with its reason. Every step that touches `web/` reads it, builds from its tokens, and adds the patterns it introduces under "Patterns", so the app stays one system. Precedence: the design brief's own words (`docs/masterplan/phases/P04-local-app.md`, "Design brief"), then this file, then the skill's values, then unslop's, then the `frontend-design` skill's defaults; the `apple-design` skill is the design reviewer's lens (D-34, D-77, D-88). Step 4.3 wrote it; D-89 gave the app its name, its logo, and its two accents.

## Direction

- **Product.** FEAT is a research terminal where a futures or crypto trader builds features, backtests them through an exact fill engine, and checks whether an edge survives, read for hours at a desk beside a charting platform.
- **Character.** Exact, dense, tech-forward: a terminal, not a desk.
- **World.** Monospaced readouts on near-black panels at night, the skill's world: pure greys with no tint, hairlines in place of shadows, and FEAT's orange, which marks where you are, what to do next, and the data that matters, with a purple for the quieter actions.
- **Moments.** The chart, a price curve in the orange with its fills marked by their sign, or a run's equity in the sign colours either side of its capital (D-119), and the figure that matters, a bold tabular number, one a page or a chart's readout one a chart; and, since step 4.4, the running jobs' bars, filling in the orange as their jobs report. The home page's other cards keep their place until the steps that build runs and features fill them.
- **Signature.** The skill's, kept whole: Geist Mono for every text, one 12 px radius on everything, and bold tabular figures on a four-step grey ramp with hairlines; FEAT's fox and wordmark at the head of the sidebar; and FEAT's identity chip, a version, hash, run key, or token name in a 12 px tag, the mark of a deterministic, pinned, hashed run (D-11).

- **Colour means something.** The chrome is grey. The orange marks the primary action, the active item, keyboard focus, a list row under the pointer, and the data that matters; the purple marks a quieter action and a link to another page, and on a Monte Carlo's charts, by the builder's exception, the run among its paths (D-119, Charts); positive and negative mark the sign of a profit or loss, an alert or error, and the confirm of an action that cannot be undone, nothing else: no red or green decoration. Log out is a neutral row, not the skill's red text, for that reason. Two exceptions are the builder's: a price chart's candles show which way a bar went in the positive and the negative, outlined by default so that they stay quieter than money, which keeps the solid colours (step 5.4, Patterns, Price chart); and in an import's review a file that imports reads Imports in the positive, while what stops a file is the negative's alert, a file imported before or empty staying quiet, as it is no error (step 5.17, D-105, Patterns, Review).
- **Lifted for contrast.** The skill's values that fail WCAG on these surfaces are lifted to the first that passes: subtle text from `#666` (3.45 : 1 on a card) to `#7c7c7c`; a field's border from `#222` (1.24 : 1) to `#666`, 3 : 1 against the card and the field (1.4.11); text on the accent in ink, `#050505`, at 7.47 : 1 on the orange, where the skill's white would give 2.73 : 1; and focus as a 2 px outline in the brand colour, 2 px outside the control, where the skill's 4 px rings in `#3d0e1f` and `#111` give 1.24 : 1 on the page. An indicator, such as a radio's dot or a progress bar, takes the brand colour too, never the fill orange, which gives 2.73 : 1 on a light card. Lifted so, subtle text sits close to body text, so meta also takes the smaller size.
- **Red apart from the orange.** Step 4.3 moved the negative to `#ff6b4f` to stand apart from the skill's pink, which leaves it 4° of hue from FEAT's orange (13°), so a loss, an error, and the confirm of an action that cannot be undone would read as the accent. The negative moves to a rose red, `#ff4d6d` on dark (349°) and `#c81e4a` on light (344.5°), 24° from the orange and 30° from the light theme's darker orange (15°), so that it never reads as the accent, as the brief asks of red. The skill's warning orange, `#f83`, sits 12° from the accent, so a warning shows in the skill's warning text colour, `#fb3`, and never in `#f83` (step 4.4's badges).
- **Hairlines and depth.** Borders are the hairline, a grey rather than an alpha. Depth comes from the surface steps and the hairlines; cards, fields, buttons, and tiles take one small neutral shadow, `0 1px 2px` in black at 45 % on dark and 6 % on light, which Tailwind's `shadow-xs` names too, so that shadcn's fields and buttons take it. ## Type

- **Geist Mono** at 400, 500, and 600 for every text, the skill's one family, and since step 5.7 at 700 for the figures that matter. It ships with the app from `@fontsource/geist-mono`, Latin subset, under the SIL Open Font License, since the content security policy allows no remote font. A monospace everywhere sets every identity and figure exactly, so the identity chip is told apart by its tag, not by its face.
- **Figures are tabular** everywhere (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on the body), in one format per column of a table, aligned right there with their decimal points in line, a figure of fewer decimals padded with spaces, which add no digit (step 5.4), while a chart's axis writes each tick with the decimals it holds (Money); a figure that matters takes 700 with -0.025em of tracking. Dates and times read `2026-11-07 14:32`, in the trader's time zone, and to the second in the trade table, `2026-11-07 14:32:05`, where two fills of one minute stand apart (step 5.8). A time a figure counts reads in its two largest units, in words, rounded to the smaller, `45 minutes`, `1 hour 30 minutes`, `3 days 4 hours`, and `Under a minute` short of half of one (`formatDuration`, step 5.22), and a spell in whole days, `12 days`.
- **Money carries its currency's sign** (D-117), so that a cash value reads as one wherever it stands: the sign before the digits and the minus before both, `$76,612.02` and `-$76,612.02`, a zero never signed; a currency without a sign follows its figure as its code, `1,250.00 USDT`, joined by a space no line breaks at, and at 12 px after a stat's figure (Stat card); and no label names the currency, "Net P&L", not "Net P&L, USD". The API's decimal text keeps every decimal it has, two at the least, and an amount a chart holds as a float reads at two, `$51,250.50`; an axis's tick or a histogram's edge keeps the decimals it holds, `$50,000`, `-$150`. A run indexed before the instrument was a setting (D-114) for a strategy built in a notebook names no currency, and its figures stand plain.
- **Hierarchy comes from size, colour, and weight together**, as the skill has it: the interface at 14 px, meta at 12 px, and only titles and figures above 16 px. Sentence case everywhere, except the two places the skill allows uppercase with open tracking, table headers and a sidebar group label. No italic, and no underline at rest; text wraps with `text-wrap-style: pretty`, which every element inherits, so that no paragraph or error ends on a lone word.

- **Measures.** Sidebar 256 px with a hairline on its right; its brand row 72 px over a hairline; navigation rows 40 px, 4 px apart, in blocks padded 16 by 12 px; the page padded 32 px, its header 32 px above the content; cards 24 px apart and padded 24 px; fields and buttons 40 px; a table's rows 40 px with 32 px controls; the content at most 1600 px wide.
- **Spacing** sits on a 4 px scale in `rem` (0.25 rem a step), so that the layout survives a larger text size: 4 between rows, 8 inside controls, 12 between an icon or a tile and its text, 16 inside the sidebar's blocks, 20 under a card's header, 24 for a card's padding and between cards, 32 for the page's padding.
- **Alignment.** Text aligns left and numbers right; icons sit in one column, labels start on one line. Only the login card is centred.
- **Radius.** One, 12 px, on everything from a card to a chip, the skill's signature: small things read as pills, large ones as rounded cards, and no second radius is added for variety. The one exception is the checkbox's 4 px (Checkbox, step 5.10), since a 16 px box with the one radius is a circle, which reads as a radio.
- **Targets.** Rows and fields of 32 to 40 px suit dense tables and navigation at a desk, which the brief asks for; the 44 px targets of the design reviewer's note wait for the phone pass (D-54).
- **Width.** Every page works at 1280 px without horizontal scroll, and what must stand on one line does so in the about 1265 px such a window leaves beside the scrollbar of a page longer than it, which the harness's browser never draws, so a spec that measures a line narrows its window to that width (step 5.4); the phone layout, the skill's "Phones" section, waits for the backlog's phone item (D-54).

## Motion

- The skill's timing: 150 ms on `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)` for hover, press, and toggles, and 200 ms for the theme's cross-fade.
- Only colour, background, border, shadow, and transform move, each named (`transition-colors`, never `transition-all`).
- A press answers on pointer-down on every control (the design brief): buttons, rows, and links scale to 0.98, and a text link turns to heading text; a radio and a colour field, too small for a scale to show, take the accent on their border, a radio's label's press included; and a row as wide as its card, such as a disclosure row or a link row, steps its surface rather than scale, which would move its far end by several pixels. A hover is one surface step, never a scale.
- The theme cross-fades on background and text colour, and only when the trader switches it; a theme painted on load switches with every transition off (`src/ui/theme.ts`), since 73 colour transitions would otherwise run at once.
- At most one deliberate motion per screen that the app starts by itself (an entrance, an automatic transition, a pulsing mark); feedback to the trader's own input does not count. The shell starts none: nothing loops, pulses, or enters on load.
- A drawer slides in from the edge it opens at and out the same way in 150 ms on transform, answering the click that opened it, and a progress bar's fill slides 150 ms on transform at each report of its job, which the brief counts as feedback too, as it does a progress bar moving. The drawer's slide is two keyframes, which restart rather than reverse when a click interrupts them, enough for a drawer a click opens, and it leaves on the curve it arrives on, where the `apple-design` lens would leave on one that speeds up: at 150 ms the two look alike (the Phase 4 exit's design review); a drawer that follows a drag, with the phone pass (D-54), needs motion that starts from where it is and keeps the release's velocity, a spring library and its decision line.
- A frame dragged by an edge follows the pointer at once, and a key moves it at once: its size and its edge's line take no transition, since an eased frame would trail the hand that holds it (Patterns, Resizable frame). A press on an edge moves nothing by itself: the line the pointer lit, the cursor, and the frame's first move are its answer.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` sets every duration to zero, the theme's cross-fade included.

## Copy

- Plain verbs, sentence case, no exclamation marks, and one spelling for one action ("Log out"). No trailing period on a label, a button, a title, or a description, which says what a page, a card, or a field holds, as the skill writes them; a message, such as an error, a warning, or an empty state, is a sentence and keeps its period.
- An action keeps its name from the button to its result: "Change password" gives "Password changed", "Revoke" gives the token gone from the list.
- Errors say what happened and what to do: "That is not FEAT's password. Type it again.", "The server did not answer: is fefat up still running?".
- A page names its place, never greets. A page not built yet says what it will do under its title, in one line, and that it is not built yet; it never shows invented data.
- Each tab names the page, then FEAT: `Lab – FEAT`. The login page shows FEAT's logo with "feature engineering for algorithmic trading" small beneath it. The code keeps the name fefat (D-89), so copy that quotes the `fefat` command or a `FEFAT_` variable keeps its spelling.

## Principles

1. **Figures first.** The page exists for its numbers and charts; the chrome stays grey so that they read at once.
2. **Colour means sign.** Red and green say whether money was made or lost, a run's equity above or below its capital among it (D-119), or that something failed, and, outlined on a price chart, which way a bar went, and in an import's review whether a file imports (D-105); the orange says where you are, what to do next, and which figure matters, and the purple what else you can do, but on a Monte Carlo's charts, where it is the run among its paths (D-119).
3. **Dense, never crowded.** Many rows on one screen, each at a readable size and contrast, grouped by space and by hairline cards.
4. **No interruptions.** Inline edits and undo for routine actions; a confirmation only where an action cannot be undone (Inline confirmation), inline rather than in a dialog, and where an import makes a choice for good, the choice itself, each option saying what it does (Decision); settings one level deep.
5. **Exactness shows.** Identities appear whole in the chip, numbers come from the API unchanged, and nothing is rounded for looks; a job's progress, a gauge rather than a result, shows as a whole percent, while its bar's `aria-valuenow` keeps the API's figure.
6. **Dark first.** The dark theme is the skill's and paints first; the light theme follows the same steps in grey and white.

## Patterns

- **Shell.** The sidebar's brand row, FEAT's logo 40 px tall, the height of the skill's logo tile, leading home; then Home, Lab, Strategies, Runs, Prop, Agent, and Data; then, under a hairline, Settings, Log out, and the version's identity chip. A row is 40 px, padded 12 px, with a 20 px lucide icon (stroke 2) 12 px from its label, both in heading text at 500; the active row takes the brand's soft fill and border, the skill's intended border that its reference lost, with its label and icon in the brand's soft foreground, and a hovered row the hairline step. The sidebar spans the window's height; its navigation scrolls inside it when the window is short, so that Settings and Log out stay in reach, and the version stays at its foot. A group label, 12 px uppercase with 0.2em of tracking in subtle text, arrives with a second group of rows; the skill's user row and quick actions wait for something to hold.
- **Logo.** FEAT's marks are the builder's drawings (D-89), one flat orange, `#fc734d`, in `public/`: `logo-full.svg`, the fox and the wordmark, 40 px tall in the brand row and on a page without the shell, and 48 px as the login card's heading; and `favicon.svg`, the fox alone on a square canvas. `index.html` preloads the logo, so that it paints with the shell rather than after it. They keep their orange in both themes, as a logo does, at 2.3 : 1 on the light sidebar, which WCAG exempts for a logotype. Each file was rewritten from the drawing program's export with plain fill attributes in place of its inline styles, which a strict content security policy can refuse, its invisible leftovers dropped and its canvas cropped to the drawing; Chromium paints it the same, pixel for pixel. The text-only wordmark waits for a place that needs it.
- **Skip link.** The skill's, the first stop of every page in the shell: hidden above the page until it has the focus, then on the hairline step with a hairline and Tailwind's middle shadow, so that it reads over the sidebar's brand row, and at once rather than with the skill's slide, since only colour, background, border, shadow, and transform move; "Skip to content" moves the focus to the page.
- **Top bar.** The skill's sticky top bar, which arrived with step 4.4's jobs indicator (D-88): 72 px over a hairline that meets the brand row's, padded 24 px, solid in the page colour rather than the reference's unblurred glass, so that nothing smears under it and reduced transparency has nothing to take away; it sits beside the sidebar and outside the page's main landmark, so that the skip link and the first tab stops stay as they were. Its controls are the skill's icon buttons, at the right: 40 px on the control surface with a hairline, the icon 20 px in heading text, the hover step under the pointer. The jobs indicator, the first, opens the jobs drawer; its count, ink on the accent fill in a 20 px pill ringed with the page colour, shows while jobs are queued or running, and its label names the counts for a screen reader (`Jobs, 1 running, 2 queued`). A job that failed or timed out while the drawer was closed puts a failure dot on the button until the drawer opens (step 5.19, D-115): the skill's notification dot, 8 px in the negative ringed with the page colour, at the button's lower right, clear of the count, and still, since nothing in the shell pulses. The label then names the failure (`Jobs, 1 running, Backtest failed`), the button's `title` is that label, the browser's own tooltip, and the drawer's Close carries one too. A polite live region, hidden but for a screen reader, says each job's end (`Backtest succeeded`). In a contrast theme of Windows the dot takes the system's highlight, and opening the drawer brings the first failure not yet seen into view. The dot lives in the open tab: a reload forgets it, and the failed job stays in the drawer. The bar's left stays empty until an action of the whole app arrives. A departure from the `apple-design` lens, which would make the chrome translucent with an edge only where the page scrolls under it: the bar is solid and its hairline shows at rest, since the hairline is the Contemporary skill's signature and meets the brand row's.
- **Page header.** The page title, then its one-line description 4 px below in body text, 32 px above the content; a page's actions (`Page`'s `action`, step 5.4) stand at the right end of the title's line, centred on it and ending at the content's right edge, 40 px buttons 8 px apart with the primary one last: Import data on Data, a link in the primary button's look, since it leads to a page, and on a dataset's page Check quality, a ghost button, before Import more data. At the desktop width the title's line, 43 px, is taller than its buttons, so a header keeps one height with or without them. A page reached from its area's page, such as the chart, the import page, the sources page, or a dataset's page from Data, leads back to it from 8 px above its title (`Page`'s `back`, step 5.4): a back link named "Back to" and that page, which leads there whatever the trader's path, from a bookmark too, where the browser's Back would step through each change of the page's address, and which is never marked as the current page. They are themed through these tokens and a few unlayered rules of `src/index.css`: on `a` and `button`, which name their transitions and give every control its press; on `:focus-visible`, the outline; and on a few `data-slot`s, which give a field its surface, height, and focus, a textarea the same surface and focus (step 5.5), a button its 40 px, the primary its hover, the ghost variant the second accent, the destructive variant the danger action, a radio its press and its dot's colour, a table room under its last row for the focus and its headers their type, a progress bar its track and fill, a sheet its panel and its slide, a link row its press without a scale and the header over it the tables' header type, and the outline variant at an icon's size the skill's icon button. These rules beat a caller's utility classes, so a control that needs another size or surface gets a rule there, keyed on a data attribute, never a class at its call site. A Button inside a trigger's `asChild`, such as the sheet's trigger and close, takes the trigger's own `data-slot`, which Radix's Slot merges over the Button's, so such a Button carries `data-slot="button"` itself, and these rules reach it.

## Review against the generic defaults

The first direction, Cobalt on an ink ramp with IBM Plex, was checked against the defaults the `frontend-design` skill lists and unslop's signs at step 4.3's plan; the builder then chose the Contemporary skill as the reference to follow exactly (D-88) and later FEAT's own name, logo, orange, and purple (D-89), and the brief's words let a reference pin a look the skills would otherwise call a default. What follows the skill, what departs from it, and why:

- **The near-black page with one bright accent** is the second default `frontend-design` names, and the skill's look. The reference pins it, so it stays with the skill's own character: pure greys with no tint, an orange that sits on the data and the active item as much as on the primary button, and bold monospaced figures. The skill's template check holds: with blue for the orange and Inter for Geist Mono this would be any dark dashboard, so the brand's own colours, the monospace, and the one radius stay.
- **A monospace for small labels** is a tell `frontend-design` names; here the reference sets every text in one monospace, so it is the system rather than a decoration.
- **Arrows after links** are another; the skill's text links carry a 16 px arrow icon only where the link leads to another page, never a `→` in the text.
- **The orange beside the sign colours.** Cobalt was chosen at the plan for its distance from red and green; the orange sits near the red, so the orange never marks a figure's sign, the red moved 24° of hue away from it (Palette), a negative figure keeps its minus sign beside its colour, and an action that cannot be undone shows the negative colour as text, never an orange fill.
- **Two accents.** The skill has one; FEAT adds a purple (D-89) for the quieter actions and the links to another page, so that the orange stays on the one thing to do and the purple says what else is there. It stays text, never a fill, and never rests on the dark hover step, but for your exception on a Monte Carlo's charts (D-119, step 5.21), where it draws the run's own path and figure as lines and marks them in the legend.
- **Contrast.** The skill's values were measured on every surface they sit on, and four were lifted (Palette, "Lifted for contrast"); the light theme, which the skill does not have, was built from the same steps and measured the same way, and the quality floor's contrast specs hold both themes.
- **The skill's own tells**, avoided: no greeting under the page title; no decorative icon tile on every card; one fill for every card and one weight for every card title; the border colour in the base layer; no surface on a surface of the same grey; one number format and one spelling per action; and no top bar until it has something to hold, then never glass without blur.
- **The brief over the skill.** Log out stays a neutral row rather than the skill's red text; every control answers a press on pointer-down, where the skill only steps the surface under the pointer; tables keep 40 px rows rather than the reference's 57 px, with a hover the reference lacks; the negative red stands 24° of hue from the orange; and the page stops at 1600 px rather than running fluid.
- **The page title** is the skill's 26 to 36 px rather than the first direction's 20 px: the reference sets the title alone above the cards, not in a toolbar's row.
